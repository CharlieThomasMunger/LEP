The institution’s status and the certificate from the individual course should be understood together.</p>
<h2>Degree level changes the next step</h2><p>BA, BSc and BBA are bachelor’s degree titles with different content. MSc is a master’s. A Diploma requires its full title and position in the relevant education system: the word alone does not identify the level.</p><p>IMI’s Graduate Diploma is a professional diploma; its MSc is a master’s. At SHMS the professional stage and bachelor completion affect time and budget. CAA’s short culinary diplomas differ from its full bachelor.</p>
<h2>Teaching country and university name need to be read together</h2><p>I am James Liu. My priority is to understand the route: where the student studies, what they learn, who validates or awards it, and which qualification they receive. A familiar foreign university name is one part of that structure.</p><p>Families need the course and intended use to make sense together. For further study, subject preparation and degree level are essential; for practical skills, the actual training matters.</p>
<h2>Plan further study before choosing the bachelor</h2><p>Identify the desired master’s and prerequisites, then examine undergraduate subjects, grades and research preparation. Hospitality business, general business and culinary management share some content but not every prerequisite. A later move into finance or data deserves early subject planning.</p>
<h2>Using the qualification in China or Europe</h2><p>Where Chinese credential evaluation is required, prepare against the Ministry of Education’s Chinese Service Center for Scholarly Exchange requirements, the particular awarding institution and actual study history. A UK or US partner degree also needs to be considered in the context of the Swiss teaching programme.</p><p>For European work, country, role, language, employer needs and the practical permit route enter the plan together. Relevant internships and professional relationships help turn study into experience.</p>
