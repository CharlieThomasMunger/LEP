**By James Liu | Lux Education Partners | Reviewed 25 September 2026**

**My view is that Swiss hospitality education deserves serious consideration for students who enjoy working with people, organising things and understanding how a business works.** Their strengths deserve a route that develops them into professional ability.

I operate businesses in Europe, visit schools and interview students. When I look at education, I ask where the learning will be useful, who will recognise the graduate’s abilities and whether the investment gives the student more choices.

## What does hospitality management actually teach?

The name may suggest reception desks, restaurants and guest rooms. The curriculum also includes accounting, marketing, revenue management, people management and projects.

At IMI, the hospitality business bachelor’s moves from operations and business accounting into consumer research, financial performance, revenue control, marketing, research and events. A hotel makes business questions tangible: why customers pay, how prices are set, how teams work and what happens when service goes wrong.

Learning cost control before working in a restaurant gives a student a different perspective on the same job. The value lies in connecting knowledge with experience.

## Why I value the ability to work with people

**My personal judgment is that connections in the physical world will become more valuable: the ability to understand people, earn trust and take responsibility deserves greater attention as machines handle more information.**

Software can help analyse a problem. Someone still has to hear a worried customer, organise a team and resolve a difficult situation. Hospitality, events and service businesses give young people repeated opportunities to develop those abilities, alongside languages, finance and analytical skills.

This should not be treated simply as a fallback for students with weaker exam results. Organising an event, noticing others’ needs and staying calm under pressure are useful strengths. They need training and discipline to become a career.

## Why Switzerland?

The attraction is how particular schools combine business teaching, practical training and industry experience. IMI’s bachelor’s brochures show three 22-week study periods, two compulsory 22-week paid internships and an optional third internship. Returning to class after working can help a student understand what they need to improve.

English-medium teaching and international classmates also create opportunities to work across cultures. Schools differ substantially in curriculum, internships and cost, so the country is the starting point; the specific programme is the decision.

| What the student hopes to do | What I examine first |
|---|---|
| Work in hotels, events or tourism | Operations, revenue management, projects and placements |
| Explore business before choosing a sector | Breadth of business teaching and room to explore through internships |
| Build a culinary or restaurant career | Kitchen training, menu costing, restaurant management and entrepreneurship |
| Join a family business | Which operating problems the course and placements can help solve |
| Continue to a master’s | Qualification, grades, research preparation and the target programme’s requirements |

A family restaurant, hotel, retail or service business can give the student somewhere concrete to use the learning. Students without a family business can build the same professional foundations, but gaining experience and relationships early becomes especially important.

## Budget, employment and residence belong in the same discussion

I see value in IMI’s combination of teaching, partner benefits and paid internships. A useful budget includes scholarships, campus accommodation and meals, placement earnings and placement living costs. It also shows when the family needs the money: wages received later cannot fund a fee due now.

For students hoping to work in Europe, planning depends on their nationality, existing residence rights, language skills and destination. A Swiss education is not itself permission to work throughout Europe. We consider those questions alongside the choice of programme.

My base is Luxembourg, with business relationships in Switzerland, the Netherlands, Ireland and Austria. I look at the markets a student may eventually enter, rather than limiting the discussion to one campus or country.
