**My judgment is clear: ordinary agencies can only reach admissions officers; becoming part of Europe’s education circles gives you direct dialogue with school heads and owners, in-depth interviews, and ongoing access to them whenever you need to get in touch. This is what I can do, and it is an important distinction between LEP and ordinary agencies.**

I have lived and operated businesses in Luxembourg for years, working continuously with schools and local companies. These relationships matter when an application needs to move forward, school resources need to be coordinated, or a family or student needs local support.

**We open and share our long-established European social network with families who work with LEP.** What matters is whom we can reach directly, why they trust us, and how those relationships can help your child.

## 1. Direct dialogue with school heads, with priority in admissions coordination and school resources

**I have deep working relationships with St George’s International School in Luxembourg and with Chantemerle, ISA Altdorf and St. Charles in Switzerland. I can communicate directly with their heads, conduct in-depth interviews and stay in touch whenever needed. In advancing admissions and coordinating school resources, I have priority access built through long-term cooperation. Many other agencies simply cannot do this. It is a significant LEP advantage.**

In my actual cooperation with these schools, admissions officers must screen applicants strictly according to school rules and handle application documents and admissions procedures. **School heads have a broader perspective and more dimensions on which to make choices: the school’s overall direction, a student’s potential and their longer-term development.**

**In-depth dialogue with school heads also helps me understand their educational philosophy and how to make use of their education, university progression and employment resources. Admissions officers do not handle matters at this level, yet families need to understand them when making longer-term decisions.**

I can discuss a child’s characteristics, family goals and future direction with the head: what resources the school has, how they can be coordinated, and how the child can actually benefit from them.

## 2. Why can I do this? My life, businesses and reputation are rooted here

I live and operate businesses in Luxembourg and also conduct business on the ground in Switzerland. My child attends an international school in Luxembourg. I consider education as a business owner, a cross-border resident and a parent.

**Luxembourg is our home base. Across Luxembourg, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Ireland and Austria, we have multiple commercial collaborations and business relationships, maintaining close contact with local companies.** These extend beyond education into commercial activity, business cooperation and local social networks, informing how I consider a child’s education alongside industry development and talent needs.

**In my experience of business and cooperation in Luxembourg, Switzerland and the Netherlands, social circles and reputation directly influence how far a relationship can develop.** Entering a circle requires sustained participation; earning trust requires keeping commitments over repeated collaborations. A school or company’s willingness to listen seriously to someone’s recommendation reflects its judgment of that person over time.

I continue to work locally. We cooperate, stay in contact, and remain accountable at the next meeting and in the next collaboration. **Trust built through long-term cooperation is a practical resource.**

### Local responsiveness turns communication into action

**I am based in Luxembourg, sharing the local working and living environment with the schools.** I can contact people directly, coordinate an in-person discussion when needed, and respond to new questions from a school by moving the next step forward with the family.

In Switzerland, the Netherlands and elsewhere, we advance discussions through established business relationships and direct contacts, arranging exchanges according to the circumstances.

**AI can help families obtain information; real-world trust has to be built by people over time.** An answer can explain an application process, but it cannot build a working relationship with a school head for a family or put a personal reputation behind an introduction to an employer. That is the value of our ongoing investment in local relationships and practical action.

## 3. Resources need to be matched to the child and the family

**We select our partner schools carefully and work only with leading schools in their particular niches. We also assess and understand students, then match them with suitable countries and schools.** Every recommendation needs a clear basis.

**LEP’s education planning incorporates the Yushufang Family Decision Framework (玉书房家庭决策体系), bringing together Chinese and Western thinking on education and personal growth with the child’s interests, demonstrated abilities, potential and family circumstances to identify suitable study directions, countries and schools.**

We explore what the child wants to commit to, what they do well, which environment suits their current language and independence, and which study and development options they want to retain for the next stage.

**The path also has to fit the family’s budget.** Tuition, living costs, accommodation, insurance, travel, parental accompaniment and subsequent bachelor’s or master’s study need to be considered together. Applicable scholarships and paid internships can be included in calculations, while distinguishing when fees must be paid from when income may arise.

Our aim is to help families identify a worthwhile path they can sustain, with room for the next stage.

## 4. Different educational stages call for different support

| Stage | Immediate family decisions | LEP’s focus | Preparation for the next stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Secondary school | Country, curriculum transition, year placement, language adjustment, boarding or parental accompaniment, budget | School dialogue, local information, applicable arrival, accommodation and guardianship coordination | Subject choices, abilities, undergraduate direction and applications |
| Bachelor’s degree | Subject and qualification, overall investment, internships, applicable scholarships | Course and application liaison, industry information, practical experience and local networks | Master’s study or employment, with relevant language and residence preparation |
| Postgraduate study | Specialisation or career change, industry direction, target employment region | Programme assessment, application dialogue, industry exchanges and suitable employer introductions | Career development, job requirements and the transition to work and residence |

### School-age families: local support and future university study

In Luxembourg, we combine local experience and school dialogue to coordinate applications, family arrival and applicable accommodation and guardianship arrangements. In the Netherlands, our current school dialogue and arrangements centre on Eerde International Boarding School. This work is considered alongside curriculum transition, adjustment and preparation for undergraduate study.

### Bachelor’s and postgraduate students: studies, progression and employment together

**Employment recommendations after graduation are a central part of LEP’s value at this stage.** I maintain long-standing relationships involving repeated face-to-face exchanges with local entrepreneurs, family offices, hotel groups and senior banking professionals in Luxembourg and Switzerland.

We have information and resources arising from real interactions, and provide employment recommendations, introductions and connections when a student and an opportunity are well matched. A valuable recommendation requires knowing both the company and the student. Career preparation should therefore develop during study, alongside courses, internships, language skills and further education plans.

## 5. Planning to work in Europe after graduation means considering residence and work arrangements early

Many families send their children to Europe hoping they will stay and work after graduation. **One important obstacle is obtaining the relevant work and residence status: even with suitable qualifications and abilities, an additional permit process can put another hurdle in the way of finding a job.**

We therefore consider education choices, career direction and residence arrangements together early on, identifying what needs preparation and confirmation at each stage. For school-age families, this also includes parental accompaniment and whether the family plans to live locally.

Deeper questions about a child’s characteristics, family goals and education choices can connect with the **Yushufang Family Decision Framework**. Further discussion of cross-border life, family arrangements and residence-related services can connect with **SBMG**. These needs belong to the same family, and education planning should understand their relationship.

## 6. Scholarships are specific benefits of some programmes

**Scholarships and partner-channel benefits are advantages we actively pursue for students on applicable programmes, not a uniform arrangement across every country and school.**

**LEP’s Swiss hospitality education partners include IMI and the schools within Swiss Education Group (SEG).** We discuss student backgrounds, application documents, partner benefits and scholarship arrangements directly with the relevant institution and programme. Each school’s conditions are explained separately.

Long-term school relationships help us understand what the school values, which student strengths to present, how to prepare documents and when to advance discussions. Families need to understand what support is available, which study stage it covers and what they will actually pay.

## 7. Families, schools, local companies and LEP grow through mutual benefit

Schools need students who fit their programmes and can develop. Local businesses need capable, trustworthy people. Families want their children to learn and gradually find their direction. LEP needs successful student development and genuine word of mouth to sustain long-term cooperation.

**Student assessment and understanding family needs → matching suitable countries and schools → student development → recognition by schools and employers → trusted recommendations → deeper cooperation and more information and opportunities → support for more well-matched families.**

This is the constructive cycle we want to build. We open our social network to families and take every recommendation to a school or company seriously, because their progress is connected with our long-term reputation.

## 8. Application services and fees, explained before we begin

**Luxembourg private international school projects require substantial local services and are fee-paying. For other partner-school applications handled by LEP, no school application service fee is charged from 2026 through 15 September 2027.**

Luxembourg private international school projects typically require ongoing school communication, family arrival and local coordination, with accommodation or guardianship arrangements depending on the family. We specify the scope and fee for the services required. For other projects, the waiver covers the school application service fee. Tuition, registration charges, visa and translation costs and other third-party fees are separate; fees for additional specialist services are also explained before work begins.
