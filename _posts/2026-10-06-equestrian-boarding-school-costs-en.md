<h2 id="section-2">Cash required is not the same as final expenditure</h2>
<p><strong>Eerde example: €61,600 + €3,650 + €2,750 = €68,000.</strong> The deposit is refundable under its terms. Riding, relevant examinations or extra support, travel and personal spending are not included in this calculation.</p><p><strong>Saint-Charles example: CHF 92,500 + CHF 300 + CHF 3,000 + CHF 2,500 = CHF 98,300.</strong> Including the CHF 5,000 deposit and CHF 7,000 account advance brings planned funds to CHF 110,300. Add CHF 12,000 if the listed language provision applies.</p><p>An account advance funds actual expenses; it is not another fixed tuition fee. If riding is charged to that account, do not count both the advance and the same riding expense twice in final expenditure. Additional funds may be needed if spending exceeds the advance.</p>

<h2 id="section-4">Ask for comparable annual arrangements</h2>
<p>Use the same assumptions for each enquiry: course, grade, weekly training, horse arrangements and likely events. Separate academic-year education, any twelve-month horse costs and one-off spending. Thirty teaching weeks of lessons cannot be compared directly with a full year of horse care.</p><p>Then consider affordability over the likely years of study. A first-year payment that can just be met is not necessarily a sustainable plan.</p>
