<p>For families interested in a Dutch bachelor’s degree, studying at an international secondary school in the Netherlands can form part of a continuous plan. Students can become familiar with local life, study in English and explore universities and subjects in person.</p>
<p>The University of Amsterdam and Erasmus University Rotterdam are examples to investigate according to the student’s interests. Both appear in Eerde’s cumulative university acceptance information for 2017–2025.</p>
<p>The benefit is earlier familiarity and preparation, not automatic admission. IB subjects, results and the requirements of the chosen degree still matter.</p>
<h2>An IB education does not tie a student to one country</h2>
<p>The IB Diploma is widely recognised across major study destinations. A student can prioritise the Netherlands while exploring universities in Switzerland, the UK, the US and elsewhere.</p>
<p>Choosing a Dutch high school does not mean committing to a Dutch university. Interests and results develop during secondary school, so it can be useful to retain several realistic directions.</p>

<figure class="article-photo"><img src="/assets/eerde/n6-2.webp" width="1600" height="1066" alt="Eerde · Classroom discussion" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Eerde · Classroom discussion</figcaption></figure><h2>Switzerland: language preparation is only one part</h2>
<p>Families interested in Switzerland can investigate institutions such as ETH Zurich and EPFL early. These universities specify IB subject combinations, levels and grades.</p>
<p>French or German preparation may be necessary for the intended course, but language cannot replace academic requirements. Studying the IB in the Netherlands does not prevent a student from preparing for a Swiss destination.</p>
<p>For a student with clear scientific strengths, this discussion is particularly useful before IB subjects are chosen.</p>
<h2>The United Kingdom and United States remain possible directions</h2>
<p>UK applications should be planned around the intended degree and relevant subject requirements. Eerde’s cumulative acceptance information includes King’s College London, illustrating that destinations are not confined to the Netherlands.</p>
<p>The US is also an option for IB students. Depending on the institution, applications may consider academic achievement, personal experiences, interests and other materials. Sustained involvement in activities the student values is more useful than assembling a last-minute collection of experiences.</p>
<p>An acceptance list describes past outcomes across multiple years. It does not predict the result for an individual student.</p>
