In October 2026, I visited both Swiss campuses of Glion Institute of Higher Education. Like Les Roches, Glion belongs to Sommet Education. The school contacted us on WhatsApp the day before, arranged discussions with academic and internship leaders and showed us both campuses. The preparation was professional and thorough.

## Glion above Montreux: scenery, dining and accommodation

At the Glion campus, the restaurant looks towards Lake Geneva and the Alps. The views were beautiful. We enjoyed roast duck at lunch, as well as good desserts. The meal made a very positive impression.

The accommodation also looked good, with dedicated student activity space. When advising a family through LEP, I consider the course alongside the room, dining and the way a student can spend an ordinary day. Glion’s overall environment felt established and carefully presented.

## Bulle: a different, more academic atmosphere

We then visited Bulle, which also felt substantial in scale, with more of a university and business-school atmosphere. **Bulle is in a different town**, and the two campuses serve different stages of study. The school describes Bulle as its main academic centre.

Seeing both helps explain how service practice connects with subsequent business learning. LEP places teaching location, accommodation, practical periods and budget on the same plan.

## Can students choose between mountain and town life?

Our student conversations touched on different preferences: some enjoy Glion’s mountain scenery, while others prefer life in Bulle. I think it is valuable for applicants to experience this difference themselves.

The actual campus arrangement depends on programme and study stage. The 2027 Swiss hospitality-business fee schedule separates the experiential stage at Glion from subsequent academic stages at Bulle. LEP communicates the student’s preference and asks which choices or changes are available for that route.

## What a student told us about practical learning

A student who hosted us said they had compared other hospitality schools and particularly valued Glion’s **practical curriculum and connection with frontline work**. This is direct student feedback that I think families should hear.

I would then ask how a young person develops from carrying out a task to understanding it: serving guests, coordinating colleagues and organising a process, followed by managing an enterprise. LEP relates the student’s interests to the programme’s actual practical work.

## Reception details reveal how people work

Our hosts were professional, courteous and warm. Academic and internship questions had appropriate people to answer them, and the itinerary was well organised.

Having run businesses for years, I pay attention to preparation, responsiveness and follow-through. These matter in hospitality and service education. The visit is one part of my assessment, considered alongside the curriculum, academic expectations and placements.

The early stage involves substantial campus-based service, food and beverage and hotel-operations practice. Company internships follow as students develop more complex responsibilities. During our discussions about Chinese students, the school described support for required internships, including destinations in Europe and the Middle East, while acknowledging the difficulty those graduates face in remaining in Switzerland for employment. LEP plans around the individual’s goals, languages and work eligibility.

## Hospitality business or luxury business?

| Route | Focus | Interests to discuss with LEP |
|---|---|---|
| International Hospitality Business | Service operations, business management and customer experience | Teams, running a business and working with guests |
| Luxury Business | Branding, marketing, finance, supply and distribution in luxury | How brands create value and serve customers |

I ask a student to describe the tasks they would enjoy: organising an experience, coordinating a team or analysing a brand’s pricing and customer development. This tells us more about motivation than simply liking luxury products.

## The full “1 + 3” route and entry assessment

The current undergraduate route describes an Experiential Year followed by three BSc years, with two internships and a period of professional practice. Location, time on campus and placement periods should be considered together.

The published minimum age is 17.5. School-leaving qualifications, relevant vocational education and work experience influence entry into the experiential stage or direct entry to the BSc. Published English requirements include IELTS 5.5 overall, with at least 5.0 in each component. LEP reviews the applicant’s transcript and experience with the school to establish the appropriate starting point and language preparation.

## The 2027 fee schedule: what CHF 232,175 covers

For the complete route in the Swiss undergraduate schedule, the listed items add up as follows. **Housing in later periods and personal expenditure still need to be budgeted separately.**

| Listed item | Full-route amount (CHF) |
|---|---:|
| Tuition | 176,500 |
| Academic resources and services | 13,600 |
| Health and accident insurance | 6,800 |
| Meal credit | 16,100 |
| Listed twin-room accommodation | 18,900 |
| Application fee | 275 |
| Total of these items | **232,175** |

A refundable damage deposit of CHF 1,250 is a separate cash requirement. The schedule prices accommodation for only some on-campus semesters. Later off-campus or placement housing, additional meal credit, transport, personal spending and room upgrades depend on the route. LEP prepares a semester-by-semester budget so families can understand payment dates and the full commitment.

## When does the larger budget make sense?

I would explore Glion closely when a student has a sustained interest in service business or luxury, is willing to invest in English, communication and practical learning, and has a family able to support the full route. LEP helps students still exploring their direction understand the real coursework and tasks before committing.

For those aiming to work in Europe, the intended occupation, country, local language and work eligibility belong in the plan from the application stage.
