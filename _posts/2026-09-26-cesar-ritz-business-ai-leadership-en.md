## Why I include CRCS in a business-school comparison

When I visit hospitality schools, I want to understand how a student will learn to run a business. CRCS connects service operations with finance, marketing, data and strategy. That combination merits a close look for students considering international service businesses, entrepreneurship or a future role in a family business.

During this Swiss trip I visited CRCS and met the Head of School. LEP works directly with CRCS, allowing us to take a family’s questions into course selection, admissions and scholarship discussions. The practical value is a course recommendation grounded in the student’s background, followed by a clear application plan.

## Bachelor’s, applied AI or master’s?

| Programme | Structure | Main consideration |
|---|---|---|
| BSc in Hospitality Business Management | Full four-year pathway, 180 ECTS and two internships | Business operations, finance, marketing and data |
| BSc in Applied AI for Hospitality Business Management | Full four-year pathway, 180 ECTS and two internships; first intake October 2027 | Applying AI to revenue, customer experience and processes |
| MSc in Leadership | 15 months, 90 ECTS | Management development after a first degree |

The business bachelor’s starts with an industry immersion year before the subsequent business curriculum. The 2027 material lists six specialisations: executive leadership, marketing and communication, international finance, business analytics, digital strategy and entrepreneurship. LEP compares these with the student’s interests and academic strengths.

The separate AI degree deserves a focused comparison: [how does CRCS applied AI differ from computer science?](/en/insights/cesar-ritz-applied-ai-degree/) Its first intake is in October 2027, so the curriculum and proposed project work are the basis for assessing it.

The master’s offers Hospitality and Tourism Management, Finance and Wealth Management, and Luxury Brand Management. Its current structure comprises three on-campus terms and two off-campus terms. The final phase offers a leadership and management capstone or a start-up accelerator, with an optional global internship.

## Entry requirements and English

Bachelor’s applicants need a completed secondary qualification. The school lists high-school diplomas, IB, A Levels and equivalent qualifications. **IELTS Academic: 5.5 overall, with no component below 4.5**; recognised alternatives are assessed against the school’s equivalencies.

The master’s normally requires a business-related bachelor’s degree. Other backgrounds may be considered individually, potentially with additional foundation learning. **IELTS Academic: 6.0 overall, with no component below 5.5.** Previous study or experience may support an assessment for credit or a different entry point to the bachelor’s pathway.

LEP organises transcripts, previous course information and English evidence before discussing entry, preparation and start dates directly with the school. Scholarship discussions build on genuine academic and personal strengths.

## What do the 2027 fees cover?

These figures come from the school’s 2027 fee schedule. They are **whole-programme school charges in Swiss francs (CHF)**.

| Component | Hospitality Business Management BSc: full four-year route | Leadership MSc: 15 months |
|---|---:|---:|
| Tuition | 88,000 | 35,500 |
| Accommodation for specified campus terms | 27,900 | 9,300 |
| Meals for specified campus terms | 13,500 | 4,500 |
| Infrastructure and services | 39,900 | 14,400 |
| **Total school charges** | **169,300** | **63,700** |

A **CHF 2,000 deposit** is separate. These figures are before any individual scholarship. LEP obtains a programme-specific quotation for the applied AI degree.

The schedule includes a deluxe single room during the specified campus terms. Meal provision covers **lunch and dinner Monday to Friday**, with equipped kitchens available for weekend use. Christmas and between-term accommodation is additional; the schedule quotes **CHF 430 per week** when reserved. Living costs during internships and other off-campus periods, travel and personal spending require a separate allowance.

LEP maps costs against the student’s actual campus, holiday and internship calendar. This gives the family a funding schedule to compare alongside course length and any scholarship offer.

## Using internships to prepare for employment

The bachelor’s includes two internships. Career services offer CV, interview and career guidance, alongside employer contact through recruitment events. The useful question is what responsibility the student can acquire, what feedback they receive and how the experience supports their next application.

We discuss an initial direction—operations, sales and marketing, revenue and analytics, or another service-business role—then consider language skills and suitable internship locations. The aim is experience that the student can explain convincingly to an employer.

CRCS holds Swiss institutional accreditation as a **University of Applied Sciences Institute**. For non-EU/EFTA students considering Switzerland, SEM describes a six-month period after graduation from a Swiss higher education institution to seek work matching their qualifications. Employment of overriding scientific or economic interest may qualify under the graduate rules; taking up the job requires the relevant authorisation. LEP brings career direction, languages, internships and permit conditions into the same planning discussion.

## Who should explore this with LEP?

CRCS merits attention for students interested in people and service who are also willing to study finance and data. A future role in a hotel, restaurant or other family service business can provide a concrete starting point for specialisation.

Students targeting technical AI research should compare computer-science and software-engineering curricula in detail. Those aiming for European employment should identify their intended sector and country early.
