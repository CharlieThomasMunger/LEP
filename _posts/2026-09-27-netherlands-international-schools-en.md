<p>Some families already live in the Netherlands and want an English-language education. Others plan to send a child while the parents remain abroad. Both may consider international schools, but their practical needs are different.</p>
<p>When parents live nearby, school choice can include day schools, commuting and after-school arrangements. When they do not, a suitable boarding arrangement must cover life outside lessons, including term-time weekends.</p>
<p>This series focuses mainly on the second group, reflecting LEP’s current Dutch school services. Other international day schools exist, but a long list of names does little for a family whose first requirement is boarding.</p>
<h2>Why we focus on Eerde</h2>
<p>Eerde International Boarding School offers full boarding, including term-time weekends. It brings education and everyday life together for students whose parents are not living locally.</p>
<p>I have visited the school and spoken directly with its staff. The combination of a historic castle estate and modern teaching facilities stood out to me. I found the boarding accommodation satisfactory and the people who welcomed us particularly warm and approachable. These first-hand impressions are part of why I believe the school is worth considering.</p>
<p>Families already in the Netherlands can also consider Eerde. They can discuss day attendance or boarding in light of their location and the child’s needs.</p>
<figure class="article-photo"><img src="/assets/eerde/n1-2.webp" width="1600" height="1066" alt="Eerde · Creative learning space" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Eerde · Creative learning space</figcaption></figure><h2>Our main application focus is age 14 and above</h2>
<p>For students moving from abroad without their parents, LEP concentrates on secondary-school applications from age 14. We consider both the appropriate academic stage and readiness to live away from home.</p>
<p>This describes our service focus, not a universal minimum boarding age. The student’s circumstances, year group and the school’s requirements still need to be considered together.</p>
<h2>English can open the route</h2>
<p>Students considering English-taught international education do not need to master Dutch before exploring schools. What matters initially is whether their English supports learning, or whether they need a preparation pathway.</p>
<p>Eerde offers different educational stages and preparation before the IB Diploma Programme. A student already learning confidently in English and one needing additional English support should not automatically be placed on the same route.</p>
<figure class="article-photo"><img src="/assets/eerde/n1-3.webp" width="1600" height="1066" alt="Eerde · Student common room" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Eerde · Student common room</figcaption></figure><h2>UWC is a different pathway</h2>
<p>UWC Maastricht also has a residential upper-secondary programme, with its own selection process. LEP has not established a partnership with UWC Maastricht, and we do not present it as a service already arranged through us.</p>
<p>Start by telling us about your child’s age, English, budget and boarding needs. We can then discuss a realistic school direction and the questions to take to Eerde.</p>

<p>LEP helps international families connect school choice with a workable application. Based in Luxembourg, we have visited Eerde, liaise directly with the school during European working hours and organise school visits in the Netherlands each quarter.</p>
