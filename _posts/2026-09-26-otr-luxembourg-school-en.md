<p class="lead">OTR feels small, friendly and approachable. Its English–French environment and the individual attention I observed are distinctive strengths. Its compact city campus is also a real difference families should understand.</p>
<figure class="article-photo"><img alt="James Liu visiting OTR. LEP photograph." loading="eager" src="/assets/fieldwork/otr.jpg"/><figcaption>James Liu visiting OTR. LEP photograph.</figcaption></figure><h2>What I saw during my visit</h2><p>OTR’s teachers were welcoming. In one French support class, a teacher worked with four or five pupils. Other language groups I learned about, including Portuguese and Japanese, also involved single-digit numbers. The small scale made a strong impression on me.</p><p>Some classes I encountered had seven or eight pupils; the school currently describes typical classes of 15–25. These are different observations, not a promise of a fixed class size. I want to know about the class a particular child would actually join.</p><p>The site is more compact than ISL or St George’s. It does not compete with them on campus space. For some children, the advantage is precisely a more familiar environment in which teachers and classmates are easier to get to know.</p><h2>English and French in daily learning</h2><p>In primary Grades 1–5, OTR describes an equal split between English and French teaching. Languages are used to learn, rather than only taught as separate subjects. This can be attractive to families building a longer-term life in Luxembourg or Europe.</p><p>A child entering a bilingual classroom needs a realistic discussion of their current languages, age and learning demands. A younger beginner and an older pupil preparing for examinations will need different plans.</p><h2>IB and Cambridge AS/A Level</h2><p>OTR offers IB programmes across PYP, MYP and DP, as well as Cambridge AS and A Level. An A Level preference does not mean that St George’s is the only school to consider. Subject availability, the pupil’s learning style and university ambitions need to be discussed with the school.</p><h2>My assessment of the fit</h2><p>OTR sits between the other two in my impression of its educational atmosphere, with its warmth and smaller scale particularly noticeable. It is worth considering for families who value bilingual development, individual discussion and a relatively lower school-fee budget among these three.</p><p>During an urgent placement search, OTR responded constructively and was willing to examine the child’s circumstances, although the family eventually chose another school. In my work with families whose parents remain abroad, its responsiveness has followed St George’s and been stronger than ISL’s.</p><h2>School and accommodation belong in one plan</h2><p>OTR is a day school.
