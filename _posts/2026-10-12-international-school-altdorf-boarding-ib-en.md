On 19 October 2026, I visited Lucerne International Academy Wikon and spoke at length with its principal. For a family looking for a small Swiss boarding school with a more manageable budget, I think it deserves serious consideration. **Its strongest points for me were value, recently renovated learning spaces and approachable teachers. Sports provision is the main trade-off to explore.**

## What is the Marienburg campus like?

The campus stands on the hill at Marienburg in Wikon. Architectural-history records describe its use as a Benedictine convent and educational institution, with modern extensions alongside the historic castle buildings. Walking through it, I found a combination of old and modern architecture, rather than the purely historic interiors a castle description might suggest.

During the visit, we learned that the campus space was about four times that of the previous site and that the relevant areas had been renovated within the last two years. Equipment looked new, teaching spaces were generous, and the overall impression was clean and comfortable.

## Around 40 pupils: what did I notice about daily life?

There were approximately 40 pupils at the time of our visit, with the existing teaching team continuing at the new location. The teachers I met were friendly. Dedicated French and German teachers were available, and our meal was enjoyable.

This scale can be appealing for a child who would benefit from a quieter setting and regular contact with teachers. LEP would then take the discussion down to the actual year group: classmates, class size, language teaching and learning support. We present the child’s current curriculum and language background to the school so that the transition can be planned properly.

## The sports facilities need a closer look

I would discuss this openly with families. The former convent’s hilltop layout did not provide space for a large collection of sports facilities. **Compared with other campuses on this trip, on-site sports provision felt relatively limited.** Tennis or golf, for example, could require facilities downhill, and the relevant organised outings were not yet running when we visited.

The school spoke about adding activities in future. For a child choosing now, I would establish the weekly activity, venue, supervision and transport that can actually be arranged. This is especially relevant for a pupil already committed to regular training.

My view is that the budget, learning spaces and interpersonal atmosphere are worth considering together. A family whose first priority is extensive on-site sport should give that requirement considerable weight in the comparison.

## How should families read the CHF55,000–60,000 annual budget?

The annual fee range discussed during this visit was approximately **CHF55,000–60,000**. It provides a useful initial budget bracket for families considering Swiss boarding education.

I judge value through the teaching spaces, staff, curriculum and everyday experience that the expenditure supports. The refurbished environment, equipment and food contributed positively to my impression, while the sports arrangements remain part of the decision.

LEP will obtain the fee breakdown for the child’s year, boarding, meals, language support and entry date, then add insurance, holidays and personal spending to the annual plan. This turns an initial budget bracket into a proposal the family can assess.

## An IB-focused route: how would your child transfer?

**Our current understanding from the visit is that IB is the school’s main curriculum direction.** I would start with the child’s existing subjects and future university interests, then discuss the entry point, subject choices, language and writing preparation.

For the IB Diploma stage, mathematics, sciences, humanities and languages need to be considered alongside possible degree choices. A pupil already studying in the UK or another country should bring details of completed subjects and examination progress; age alone does not determine the appropriate transfer year.

LEP will liaise with the school using recent reports, subjects, English proficiency and the intended entry date. We can discuss the actual year group, available subjects and French or German support, and arrange a visit so the pupil can connect the academic plan with their own impression of the campus.

## If parents are abroad, what needs to be arranged?

Beyond the room and meals, the family needs a workable weekend and holiday plan, a clear point of contact when a problem arises, and a reliable way of communicating with the school. Off-site sport and transport are particularly relevant here.

Having seen the campus and spoken with the principal, LEP can bring a child’s age, curriculum, languages, independence, sports habits and budget into that discussion. School selection then becomes a plan for learning and everyday life.
